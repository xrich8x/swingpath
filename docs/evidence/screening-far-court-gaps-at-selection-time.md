# Screening far-court gaps at SELECTION time

> Evidence for the `screening-far-court-gaps-at-selection-time` row in [docs/STATE.md](../STATE.md) (What has not worked).
> Text preserved verbatim from SCOREBOARD.md at the 2026-08-26 split.

**GATE FAILS under cross-validation, and the null control is what makes it readable.** 41% of queued gaps contain no findable ball, so a screen must run before the human spends the effort — it cannot be the anchor control, which needs a human click by construction. Scored 7 queue-computable features on **79 gaps with a human verdict** (gate: keep ≥70% of usable, drop ≥60% of unusable). Best single feature: `anchor_disp_px`, keep 73.0% / drop **50.0%**. Every median is ordered the *right* way (usable gaps have more displacement, roam and vertical drop) and every distribution overlaps too much to act on. Sweeping **pairs** produces **569 passing combinations** — which is what ~500,000 candidate rules do to 79 points: cross-validated on the natural round split, **0–3% survive** (held-out keep 52.6–62.2%, drop 51.2–58.4%). **The null control is the useful part: the same search on SHUFFLED labels yields 0 passing pairs**, so the signal is real and simply far too weak to screen on. Also ruled out for free: the midpoint's tracker prior is **pure linear interpolation of its anchors (0 of 49 deviate >0.5 px)**, so any "is the midpoint consistent" feature is identically zero. Session J's roam criterion retested at n=79 (was 12) and still fails. **Fourth independent failure to make the far-court queue reliable** — the lever is blocked on an unsolved problem, not on labelling effort.
