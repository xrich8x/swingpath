# P/R/F1 at the field's threshold, and it changes a conclusion I shipped hours earlier

> Evidence for the `p-r-f1-at-the-field-s` row in [docs/STATE.md](../STATE.md) (What has worked).
> Text preserved verbatim from SCOREBOARD.md at the 2026-08-26 split.

Review finding P1-2: every number here was a **hit-rate at tau=10 px** while the ball literature (WASB, TrackNetV3) reports **F1 at tau=4 px** — not comparable on either axis, and nothing said so. `tools/eval_gold.py` now prints precision/recall/F1 at both thresholds, counting a lock >tau from the click (`wrong`) AND any lock on a no-ball frame as false positives. **It reverses the P1-1 verdict at the strict threshold.** Pooled over the same 10 clips and caches: F1@4 **tracknet 57.8 / ours 48.5 / wasb 47.5**, and per clip TrackNet wins **9 of 10** while BallNet wins **0**; at tau=10 BallNet's lead is **+0.5 F1**, a tie rather than the +2.9 hit-rate margin. The shape is diagnostic, not noise — BallNet has the **best recall** of the three at both thresholds (66.5 / 77.5) and the **worst precision** (38.2 / 66.2): it finds the ball more often and localises it less precisely. So the fair description is **a recall-first detector the suppression chain exists to clean up**, not a detector that beats TrackNet. Caveat held constant: these caches skip `suppress_false_locks`/`smooth_forecast` for all three models equally, so the comparison is fair but the absolute precision is not the shipped figure. **AP deliberately NOT reported** — it needs a per-detection confidence to sweep and the caches store positions only; emitting an AP-shaped number from thresholded data would be inventing a metric.
