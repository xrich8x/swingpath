# Motion attention (TrackNetV4)

> Evidence for the `motion-attention` row in [docs/STATE.md](../STATE.md) (What has not worked).
> Text preserved verbatim from SCOREBOARD.md at the 2026-08-26 split.

**CONCLUSION STANDS, and it has now been tested on the RIGHT population — the original evidence was not.** Session F skipped it because "it suppresses *static* confusers; ours **move** (59.2% travel with a person)" — but that 59.2% was measured on RAW detector locks, the population Session F itself declared *"not the product"*. Session I then found the 5 universal chain-surviving ghosts are **3 static / 2 person-attached**, the opposite split, so the dismissal looked like it had been scored on the wrong population. **RE-MEASURED 2026-08-15 using MotionPrompt's own criterion** (mean \|f0−f1\|+\|f1−f2\| in a patch at each lock), on the 20 SOLID ghosts that survive the whole chain and get drawn on a human no-ball frame, referenced against the same statistic at **532 human ball clicks**: real balls read p10 **1.17** / median 3.32 / p90 11.45; **only 5 of 20 ghosts (25%)** fall below the real-ball 10th percentile. **15 of 20 sit on things moving as much as a genuine ball.** So a motion gate tuned not to damage the dimmest decile of real sightings reaches **a quarter** of the problem. **Not worth the training run** — and this directly contradicts an independent review that called it "the single highest-value-per-hour item". NOTE the infrastructure is complete and unused: `_ballnet.MotionPrompt` is wired, `train_ballnet.py --motion-attention` exists, and **0 of 8 shipped weight files contain `motion.*` keys** — it has never once been trained. n=20 ghosts is small (trap 9), but it is 4x the n=5 the counter-argument rested on and the direction is unambiguous.
