The "beats TrackNet" claim in `pipeline.py`'s auto-select comment traced to numbers from before v21 (+57% data, hard-negative mining) and was **UNVERIFIED as current** per the review. Re-ran the existing harness (`ball_perception.py` + `eval_gold.py`, no new tool) across all 10 gold clips, one model at a time on the GPU: pooled hit@10 **BallNet 60.8% vs TrackNet 57.9% vs WASB 49.3%** — BallNet still wins on hit-rate, but by **+2.9 pts over TrackNet, not the +10.5 this comment used to claim** — **and hit-rate turns out to be the flattering metric. CORRECTED same day with F1** (see the row below): F1@10 is a **tie** (71.4 vs 70.9) and at the field's **tau=4 px TrackNet leads by 9.3 F1 points and wins 9 of 10 clips, BallNet none**. Not a clean win: TrackNet beats BallNet outright on 2 of 10 clips, and BallNet false-fires the most under identical (non-suppressed) tracker gating (55.1% vs 31.5%/40.1% — **not comparable to the shipped ~6.0% pooled false-fire**, which is measured AFTER `suppress_false_locks`/`smooth_forecast`, absent here by design so the three detectors are compared on equal footing). `pipeline.py`'s comment now cites this dated evidence instead of an unsourced number; the stale "live-ball filter" reference in the same comment (retired E5+) is also corrected to `suppress_false_locks`. Two hiccups during the run, both diagnosed and fixed rather than papered over: `yt_match40` (native 29fps) rejected `--target-fps 30` as exceeding source — switched to the project's own `--frame-step` formula; `gold_L73ep7JHiJ4`'s tracknet cache was transiently rebuilt under a different decimation scheme than its wasb/ours caches (100-frame n_ball mismatch caught by comparing raw counts, not just percentages) and rebuilt to match.
